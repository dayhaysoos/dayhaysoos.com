The idea of starting or finishing a personal project probably stresses you out. You probably don't feel like you're skilled enough to ship something, or no one would care about your project or maybe you're flat our burnt out after work. I'm here to tell you that your personal project the best way to take control of your own growth and maybe even your own life. 

Instead of thinking of personal projects as some kind of burden, I like to think of them as some form of self-care. Not the nice, relaxing, pampering form of self-care, more like an uncomfortable but very rewarding form self-care, kinda like going to the gym.

### "What if I can't think of a good personal project?"

Not that I'm some kinda pessimist, but I'm of the belief that everything sucks (except for Avatar: The Last Airbender, the only thing that actually doesn't suck). You will never be able to fully solve the things that suck, however, you certainly can make them _suck less_.

So, if you can't think of a solid personal project, why don't you think of something that sucks. It could be anything. How long the wait is at the bus stop, maybe the UI of your favorite app is too cluttered or perhaps your coffee-making process isn't automated enough. What's important is that you need to think about things that suck specifically _for you._ You would be much more motivated to solve your own problems than problems for someone else. It just happens to be that usually, you will make something that many people will be happy with, but that's more or less a side effect.

Now that you've thought about something that sucks, figure out how you can use your coding abilities to make that thing suck less.
